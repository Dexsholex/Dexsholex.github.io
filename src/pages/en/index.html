Counting oil palms from aerial imagery

For a commercial oil palm plantation in West Africa, a YOLOv8 model finds each palm crown in aerial imagery and turns it into a mapped, countable tree, block by block. On held-out test tiles it counted {{ palm.model_count }} palms where a hand count found {{ palm.hand_count }}.

Process

How I work

  1. Scoping call

    A 15- or 30-minute call: you tell me the decision you need to make, and I tell you whether spatial data can help and what it would take.

  2. Pilot on your data

    I test the approach on a small part of your area and report its error rate, so you can judge it before you commit.

  3. Delivery you keep

    You get the results, the code that produced them and documentation your team can follow.

Engagements

Ways to work together

Pick the shape that fits your budget and how sure you are of the approach.

  • Pilot

    A fixed-scope test on part of your area. You see the method, the output and its error rate before you commit to the full job.

  • Project

    A defined deliverable, such as a tree inventory, a flood screening or a web map, against a written scope, timeline and acceptance criteria.

  • Monitoring

    The same analysis repeated monthly, by season or by year, for programmes and portfolios that need to track change.

  • Advisory and strategy

    Strategic and technical advice by the day or month: what to build or buy, a review of your data and methods, or a geospatial specialist for your bid.

  • Training

    Workshops built around your team's own data, in person in Nigeria or online.
